    Datasets and tools¶

    TL;DR

    • Free data is fine for learning and for checking published factor claims (French library, AQR, Open Source Asset Pricing, FRED). It is not fine for trading decisions: most free price data is survivor-only, back-adjusted and not point-in-time.
    • Professional research runs on licensed data (CRSP, Compustat, TAQ, OptionMetrics, usually through WRDS) and on direct-feed captures for anything intraday. The licence often costs more than the compute and limits what you may do with results.
    • The Python stack covers almost everything below HFT: pandas or polars plus DuckDB for data, statsmodels and scikit-learn for models, cvxpy for optimization, and zipline-reloaded or NautilusTrader for backtests. Its blind spots are purging and embargo in CV, costs and impact, and corporate actions.
    • Every dataset below has a "what goes wrong" note. Read those before the description.

    Level: Core · Read time: ~12 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    The four ways data lies¶

    Problem What goes wrong Typical symptom Fix KB page
    Survivorship bias Universe built from stocks alive today; delisted losers vanish Small-cap and value backtests look too good Security master with dead tickers; delisting returns Point-in-time data and cleaning
    Look-ahead bias Restated fundamentals, revised macro, today's index membership Fundamental signals work "too well" around reporting dates As-first-reported values stamped with filing or release dates Backtest pitfalls
    Corporate actions Splits, dividends, spinoffs, ticker changes mishandled Fake jumps, wrong returns, broken pairs Permanent IDs (e.g., CRSP PERMNO) and total-return adjustment Point-in-time data and cleaning
    Licensing Academic licence used commercially; vendor data redistributed Legal exposure, unusable research Read the licence before the data Alternative data 
    flowchart LR
    V["Vendor history<br/>as seen today"] --> SM["Security master<br/>incl. dead tickers"]
    SM --> PIT["Point-in-time store<br/>as-known timestamps"]
    PIT --> ADJ["Corporate-action<br/>adjusted returns"]
    ADJ --> BT["Backtest"]
    T1["Survivorship"] -.-> SM
    T2["Look-ahead"] -.-> PIT
    T3["Licence terms"] -.-> BT

    Each stage removes one class of error. Skipping any one of them is enough to invalidate a backtest.

    How to read an entry. ★ means you can download and use it in an afternoon. ★★ needs cleaning or a licence. ★★★ needs serious engineering (raw feeds, terabytes). Versions and URLs were checked in 2026-11. Paid products are labelled; prices are not quoted because they are negotiated.

    Free factor and anomaly data¶

    • [Verified] French, K. R. (n.d.). Data Library (Fama-French factors, momentum and reversal factors, industry and characteristic-sorted portfolios; US, developed and emerging). Tuck School of Business. https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The default factor-return source, and the source of most factor figures computed in this KB. What goes wrong: returns are gross of costs and paper portfolios. Value is rebalanced annually. Series are rebuilt from CRSP and Compustat each update, so history can change between downloads; save dated copies. Backs Factor investing fundamentals · Momentum.
    • [Verified] AQR Capital Management (n.d.). Datasets (Betting Against Beta, Quality Minus Junk, Value and Momentum Everywhere, Time Series Momentum, Century of Factor Premia and others). https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Long monthly histories of AQR-defined factors across asset classes. What goes wrong: the definitions are AQR's own (for example, its HML variant uses current prices), and the publisher runs products on these ideas. Backs Value, quality and low volatility · Carry and cross-asset risk premia.
    • [Verified] Chen, A. Y. & Zimmermann, T. (2026-10). Open Source Asset Pricing: data (October 2026 release). https://www.openassetpricing.com/data/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Portfolio returns and firm-level signals for 200+ published predictors, plus code that rebuilds them from CRSP and Compustat if you have WRDS access. What goes wrong: releases are revised, including an October 2024 look-ahead fix, so pin the release you used. Backs Feature engineering · Overfitting and multiple testing.
    • [Verified] Jensen, T. I., Kelly, B. & Pedersen, L. H. (n.d.). Global Factor Data. https://jkpfactors.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Factor returns for the 153 factors and 93 countries of Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023), grouped into themes. What goes wrong: like all factor data it is gross of costs, and many country-level series are thin early on. Backs Factor investing fundamentals.

    Licensed research databases (paid)¶

    • [Verified] Wharton Research Data Services (n.d.). WRDS. https://wrds-www.wharton.upenn.edu/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · The gateway most academics and many funds use for CRSP, Compustat, TAQ, OptionMetrics, I/B/E/S and more. What goes wrong: access is by institutional subscription, and academic licences generally bar commercial use. Code written against WRDS tables does not run elsewhere. Backs Point-in-time data and cleaning.
    • [Verified] CRSP (n.d.). CRSP US Stock Databases. https://www.crsp.org/research/crsp-us-stock-databases/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Survivorship-free US daily and monthly prices, returns and shares, with permanent IDs (PERMNO, PERMCO). The site now redirects to Morningstar Indexes. What goes wrong: missing delisting returns need imputation. The 2025 move from the legacy SIZ format to CIZ changed how dividends enter returns and rewrote a share of historical monthly returns, so old and new extracts disagree. Backs Point-in-time data and cleaning.
    • [Verified] S&P Global Market Intelligence (n.d.). Compustat Financials. https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/solutions/products/compustat-financials (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Standardized fundamentals back to the 1950s for US firms. What goes wrong: the standard files show restated values, and report dates must be lagged or taken from a point-in-time product; otherwise you trade on numbers nobody had. Backs Fundamental quant signals.
    • [Verified] OptionMetrics (n.d.). IvyDB (historical options data). https://optionmetrics.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · End-of-day US option prices, implied volatilities, Greeks and fitted surfaces, standard in academic options research. What goes wrong: end-of-day mids are not tradable prices, and closing quotes on illiquid strikes are stale. Intraday vol strategies need intraday data. Backs Volatility risk premium and vol arb · Options pricing and vol surfaces for MM.

    Trades, quotes and order books¶

    • [Verified] NYSE (n.d.). Daily TAQ (trades and quotes). https://www.nyse.com/data-products/catalog/daily-taq (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Every consolidated US trade and NBBO quote, the standard source for spreads, TCA studies and intraday research. What goes wrong: it is the SIP view, which is slower than direct feeds. Trade-condition codes, late prints and odd lots need filtering, and the files are terabyte-scale. Backs NBBO and market data · Transaction cost analysis.
    • [Verified] Nasdaq (n.d.). TotalView-ITCH sample files. https://emi.nasdaq.com/ITCH/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Free full-day binary samples of Nasdaq's order-by-order feed. Parsing one is the best way to understand a limit order book; the format is in the ITCH 5.0 specification. What goes wrong: it covers one venue only, while the stock trades on many, and a few sample days cannot support a backtest. Backs Order-book and trade-flow signals · Lab 01.
    • [Verified] LOBSTER (n.d.). Limit order book data reconstructed from Nasdaq ITCH, with free samples. https://lobsterdata.com/info/DataSamples.php (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Ready-made message and order-book files to N levels, priced for academics. It is the easiest route to real book data for OFI or microprice experiments. What goes wrong: it shows the Nasdaq visible book only, without queue position at other venues. Backs Fair value and microprice · Queue position and order placement.
    • [Verified] Cboe Global Markets (n.d.). Cboe DataShop. https://datashop.cboe.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Historical options, equities and volatility-index data, mostly paid. VIX and SPX daily history files are free on Cboe's site. What goes wrong: options datasets differ in quote snapshot times and in whether they include all exchanges. Check before comparing to OptionMetrics. Backs Options market making.
    • [Verified] Databento (n.d.). Market data API. https://databento.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · A commercial vendor of normalized historical and live exchange data, including order-book-level schemas, with usage-based access. What goes wrong: exchange licence fees and redistribution terms still apply on top of the vendor's price. Backs Backtesting engines.
    • [Verified] FINRA (n.d.). Short Sale Volume Data (daily). https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/short-sale-volume-data (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Daily volume of trades marked short on FINRA facilities. What goes wrong: it is routinely misread as short interest. Most of it is market makers' and arbitrageurs' hedging short sales, so a "50% short volume" figure says little about bearish bets. Backs Shorting, financing and settlement.
    • [Verified] FINRA (n.d.). OTC Transparency Data (ATS and non-ATS volume by firm). https://otctransparency.finra.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Weekly off-exchange volume by ATS and by wholesaler, published with a lag of a few weeks. It is the best public view of who internalizes retail and institutional flow. Backs Venues and retail flow · Retail flow internalization.

    Macro and filings¶

    • [Verified] Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (n.d.). FRED. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Hundreds of thousands of macro and market series, including VIX, rates and FX, with an API. What goes wrong: FRED shows the latest revised values. For any macro signal, use ALFRED below. Backs Regime detection.
    • [Verified] Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (n.d.). ALFRED (archival FRED vintages). https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Every past vintage of a series, i.e., what GDP or payrolls looked like on the day. This is the only honest way to backtest a macro signal. Backs Backtest pitfalls.
    • [Verified] U.S. SEC (n.d.). EDGAR application programming interfaces (XBRL company facts and submissions on data.sec.gov). https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Free JSON of every XBRL financial fact, each stamped with its filing date and accession number, so you can rebuild what was reported and when. What goes wrong: XBRL tags vary across firms and years. The SEC throttles clients that do not declare a user agent, and full-text NLP still needs the filings themselves. Backs Fundamental quant signals · NLP and LLM signals.

    Competition datasets (Kaggle)¶

    Good for practising validation discipline on real but anonymized data. Bad as evidence of what works: features are obfuscated, windows are short, public leaderboards reward overfitting, and competition rules restrict use of the data.

    • [Reported] Optiver (2021). "Optiver Realized Volatility Prediction." Kaggle competition. https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-realized-volatility-prediction (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Short-horizon realized volatility from order-book and trade snapshots. A good first exercise in book features. Backs Order-book and trade-flow signals.
    • [Reported] Optiver (2023). "Optiver – Trading at the Close." Kaggle competition. https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/optiver-trading-at-the-close (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Predicts Nasdaq closing-auction price moves from auction imbalance and book data. It is the closest public dataset to closing-auction trading. Backs Auctions and closing liquidity.
    • [Reported] Jane Street (2020). "Jane Street Market Prediction." Kaggle competition. https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jane-street-market-prediction (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Anonymized features and a trade/no-trade utility score. It is a lesson in how little a well-validated model can extract. Backs Time-series cross-validation.
    • [Reported] Jane Street (2024). "Jane Street Real-Time Market Data Forecasting." Kaggle competition. https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/jane-street-real-time-market-data-forecasting (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Forecasts anonymized "responders" with live-updated scoring, so non-stationarity is built into the evaluation. Backs ML-driven short-horizon alpha.
    • [Reported] Two Sigma (2018). "Two Sigma: Using News to Predict Stock Movements." Kaggle competition. https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/two-sigma-financial-news (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Market data plus licensed news analytics, from the pre-LLM era. Useful for seeing how hard news signals are to monetize. Backs NLP and LLM signals.

    Data handling and modelling tools¶

    • [Verified] pandas development team (2026). pandas 3.1.1. https://pandas.pydata.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The default DataFrame library, BSD-licensed. Watch: the 3.x series changed defaults such as copy semantics, so pin versions. Index alignment silently creates look-ahead when you shift the wrong way. Backs Feature engineering.
    • [Verified] Polars (2026). polars 2.1.0. https://pola.rs/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · A multi-threaded, lazy DataFrame engine (MIT) that is much faster than pandas on wide panels and tick data. Watch: a different API, and fewer finance-specific helpers. Backs Point-in-time data and cleaning.
    • [Verified] DuckDB contributors (2026). DuckDB 2.0. https://duckdb.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · An in-process SQL engine that queries Parquet files directly. It is a good local substitute for a tick database at research scale. Backs Trading and research tech stacks.
    • [Verified] statsmodels developers (2026). statsmodels 0.15.0. https://www.statsmodels.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · OLS with HAC (Newey-West) errors, ADF and Engle-Granger cointegration tests, state-space models (BSD). Watch: there is no built-in Fama-MacBeth, and the default OLS errors are wrong for overlapping returns. Backs Pairs trading and cointegration · Evaluating signals.
    • [Verified] scikit-learn developers (2026). scikit-learn 1.9.1. https://scikit-learn.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Regularized linear models, trees, pipelines and CV utilities (BSD). Watch: TimeSeriesSplit offers only a fixed gap, not label-aware purging or embargo, so overlapping labels leak; see the CV card. Backs Time-series cross-validation · Linear models and regularization.
    • [Verified] LightGBM developers (2026). LightGBM 4.7.0. https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Gradient-boosted trees, the workhorse for tabular return prediction, alongside XGBoost. Watch: early stopping on a randomly drawn validation set leaks across time, so validate on a later period. Backs Tree ensembles.
    • [Verified] Sheppard, K. (2026). arch 9.0.0. https://bashtage.github.io/arch/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · GARCH-family volatility models, unit-root tests and block and stationary bootstraps. It is the right tool for resampling serially dependent returns. Backs Volatility risk premium and vol arb · Overfitting and multiple testing.

    Optimization and portfolio tools¶

    • [Verified] CVXPY developers (2026). CVXPY 1.9.3. https://www.cvxpy.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · A convex-optimization modelling language (Apache-2.0), the engine behind the KB's MVO lab. Watch: solver choice matters for speed and accuracy at 3,000 names, and commercial solvers such as MOSEK or Gurobi need licences. Backs Mean-variance optimization · Lab 14.
    • [Verified] Busseti, E., Boyd, S. and contributors (2026). Cvxportfolio 1.5.0. https://www.cvxportfolio.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · The reference implementation of Boyd et al.'s multi-period trading framework, with costs, holding costs and back-testing in one object model. Backs Multi-period optimization and trading toward the aim.
    • [Verified] Riskfolio-Lib developers (2026). Riskfolio-Lib 7.4.1. https://riskfolio-lib.readthedocs.io/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Many risk measures (CVaR, drawdown-based), risk parity, HRP and HERC on top of CVXPY (BSD). Watch: the many options make it easy to overfit the choice of risk measure. Backs Alternatives to MVO.
    • [Verified] Martin, R. A. and contributors (2026). PyPortfolioOpt 1.6.0. https://pyportfolioopt.readthedocs.io/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Readable MVO, Black-Litterman, HRP and Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage (MIT). Good for learning, thin on costs and constraints. Backs Covariance estimation and shrinkage.
    • [Verified] skfolio developers (2026). skfolio 1.12.9. https://skfolio.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Portfolio optimization, factor models and risk tools with a scikit-learn API (BSD), so allocation methods can be cross-validated like models. Backs Alternatives to MVO · Time-series cross-validation.
    • [Verified] QuantLib developers (2026). QuantLib 1.44. https://www.quantlib.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · A C++ pricing library with Python bindings covering curves, options, vol surfaces and calendars (BSD). It is accurate and verbose. Backs Options pricing and vol surfaces for MM.

    Backtesting and research platforms¶

    • [Verified] Jansen, S. and contributors (2026). zipline-reloaded 3.2.1. https://zipline.ml4trading.io/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · The maintained fork of Quantopian's event-driven daily and minute backtester (Apache-2.0). Watch: results are only as survivorship-free as the data bundle you load, and its built-in slippage models are simple and uncalibrated. Backs Backtesting engines.
    • [Verified] Jansen, S. and contributors (2026). alphalens-reloaded 0.5.1. https://alphalens.ml4trading.io/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Factor tear sheets: IC by horizon, quantile returns, turnover. It is a fast first look at a signal. Its companion pyfolio-reloaded does strategy tear sheets. Backs Evaluating signals.
    • [Verified] Polakow, O. (2026). vectorbt 1.2.1. https://vectorbt.dev/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Vectorized backtesting fast enough to sweep thousands of parameter sets. That is also its danger: it makes multiple testing effortless. It ships under a "fair-code" licence; read it before commercial use. Backs Overfitting and multiple testing.
    • [Verified] Nautech Systems (2026). NautilusTrader 1.231.0. https://nautilustrader.io/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · An event-driven engine with a Rust core and Python API (LGPL-3.0), designed to run the same strategy code in backtest and live trading, down to order-book data. Backs Backtesting engines · Anatomy of an HFT trading loop.
    • [Verified] QuantConnect (n.d.). LEAN engine. https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/lean-engine (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · An open-source C#/Python engine behind a hosted platform with bundled data. Watch: the convenient hosted data sets the limits of your research. Backs Backtesting engines.
    • [Verified] Manoim, G. and contributors (2026). exchange-calendars 4.13.2. https://pypi.org/project/exchange-calendars/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Trading sessions, holidays and early closes for many exchanges. Off-by-one-day bugs around half-days and holidays are a classic source of look-ahead. Backs Backtest pitfalls.
    • [Verified] KX (n.d.). kdb+ and the q language. https://kx.com/products/kdb/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · A columnar time-series database with a vector language. Its Python interface (PyKX, now documented as KDB-X Python) is under a proprietary licence. It is widely described as the standard tick store at banks and trading firms [Lore]. Watch: commercial licensing and q's learning curve. DuckDB and Parquet cover much research-scale work. Backs Trading and research tech stacks.
    • [Verified] Backtrader (2023). backtrader 1.9.78.123. https://pypi.org/project/backtrader/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Listed as a warning: still common in tutorials, but its last PyPI release was in April 2023. Prefer the maintained engines above for new work. Backs Backtesting engines.
