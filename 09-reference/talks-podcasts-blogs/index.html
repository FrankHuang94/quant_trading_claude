    Talks, podcasts and blogs¶

    TL;DR

    • Firms say more in podcasts, engineering blogs and conference talks than in any filing. Jane Street's Signals and Threads and Optiver engineers' CppCon talks are the best primary sources on how a trading firm is actually built.
    • Firm research blogs (AQR, Man Group, Two Sigma) are high quality but written by asset managers with products to sell. Read them as well-argued advocacy, and look for the paper behind each chart.
    • For staying current: Quantocracy aggregates, Flirting with Models interviews, the Open Source Asset Pricing project lets you check factor claims yourself, and arXiv q-fin.TR is where microstructure research appears first.
    • Every URL below was checked in 2026-11. Some sites block automated checkers but load in a browser.

    Level: Core · Read time: ~8 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    How to read an entry. ★ means general audience; ★★ assumes you know the KB's vocabulary; ★★★ assumes engineering or graduate-level math. Firm-produced content is primary evidence about that firm's views and technology. It is not evidence that a technique works, and never evidence about other firms.

    Podcasts¶

    • [Verified] Jane Street (2020–). Signals and Threads, hosted by Ron Minsky. https://signalsandthreads.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Long-form conversations with Jane Street engineers and researchers. The tone is engineering-first and the content is unusually concrete for a trading firm. Start with the four episodes below. Backs Anatomy of an HFT trading loop · Trading and research tech stacks.
    • [Verified] Jane Street (2020-09-09). "Programmable Hardware with Andy Ray." Signals and Threads, episode 1. https://signalsandthreads.com/programmable-hardware/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Why and where a trading firm puts logic on FPGAs, and what hardware design culture looks like there. Backs How infrastructure constrains algorithms · Semiconductor lens.
    • [Verified] Jane Street (2020-09-23). "Multicast and the Markets with Brian Nigito." Signals and Threads, episode 3. https://signalsandthreads.com/multicast-and-the-markets/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · How exchanges handle huge message rates with single-threaded matching engines and multicast market data. It is the best audio primer on the direct feed world. Backs NBBO and market data · The limit order book.
    • [Verified] Jane Street (2020-10-14). "Clock Synchronization with Chris Perl." Signals and Threads, episode 6. https://signalsandthreads.com/clock-synchronization/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Why every host's clock must agree to within about 100 microseconds, and be proven to, before latency, markouts or regulatory timestamps mean anything. Backs Adverse selection and markouts · Colocation, networks and the speed of light.
    • [Verified] Jane Street (2025-03-10). "Finding Signal in the Noise: Machine Learning and the Markets with In Young Cho." Signals and Threads, episode 22. https://signalsandthreads.com/finding-signal-in-the-noise/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · ML in a low-data, high-noise, regime-shifting setting, the move from linear models to deep nets, and the tension between research tools and production systems. Backs ML-driven short-horizon alpha · Neural nets and sequence models.
    • [Verified] Hoffstein, C. (host) (n.d.). Flirting with Models. https://www.flirtingwithmodels.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Interviews with people who design systematic strategies (trend, factors, volatility, portfolio construction), still running in its seventh season in 2026. The research library at https://blog.thinknewfound.com/ is a useful companion. Backs Trend following and CTAs · Factor timing, crowding and quant winters.
    • [Verified] Kaastrup-Larsen, N. (host). Top Traders Unplugged. https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · A long-running podcast centred on CTAs and trend followers, with recurring "systematic investor" panels. It is useful for how managed-futures managers explain drawdowns to clients. Backs Trend following and CTAs.

    Conference talks and seminar series¶

    • [Verified] Cook, C. (2017). "When a Microsecond Is an Eternity: High Performance Trading Systems in C++." CppCon 2017 (Optiver). https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NH1Tta7purM (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · The classic low-latency talk. It covers the hot path, cache warming, avoiding allocation and branching, measuring tick-to-trade, and the risk rule "if in doubt, pull your orders". Slides are on the CppCon GitHub. Backs Anatomy of an HFT trading loop · Algorithmic risk controls.
    • [Verified] Gross, D. (2024). "When Nanoseconds Matter: Ultrafast Trading Systems in C++." CppCon 2024. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sX2nF1fW7kI (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · A modern companion to Cook's talk, on data-structure and concurrency design for a trading system's hot path. Backs Trading and research tech stacks · Order types and auctions.
    • [Verified] CppCon (n.d.). CppCon YouTube channel. https://www.youtube.com/@CppCon (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Search it for "trading" and "low latency": several firms present every year. Treat each talk as one firm's practice, not the industry's. Backs How infrastructure constrains algorithms.
    • [Verified] Jane Street (n.d.). Tech Talks. https://www.janestreet.com/tech-talks/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Recorded engineering talks by Jane Street staff and guests. With the tech blog, it is the primary source on the firm's OCaml-centred stack. Backs Trading and research tech stacks.
    • [Verified] The Microstructure Exchange (n.d.). Online seminar series. https://microstructure.exchange/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Recorded academic seminars on market microstructure, often working papers a year or two before publication. Backs Equity market making · Latency arbitrage.

    Firm research and engineering blogs¶

    • [Verified] Jane Street (n.d.). Jane Street Tech Blog. https://blog.janestreet.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Engineering posts, mostly about OCaml, build systems and tooling. Useful for culture and stack and silent on strategies, as you would expect. Backs Trading and research tech stacks.
    • [Verified] Hudson River Trading (n.d.). HRT Beat. https://www.hudsonrivertrading.com/hrtbeat/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · HRT's engineering and research blog. The site may block automated checkers. Backs Trading and research tech stacks.
    • [Verified] Two Sigma (n.d.). Insights and articles. https://www.twosigma.com/insights/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Data-science and factor-risk articles, including material on its factor-lens framework. Backs Risk models · Factor investing fundamentals.
    • [Verified] Man Group (n.d.). Man Insights (including the Man Institute). https://www.man.com/insights (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Research notes from a large systematic manager on trend following, volatility targeting, trading costs and ML. Backs Trend following and CTAs · Position sizing and risk budgeting.
    • [Verified] AQR Capital Management (n.d.). Research library. https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Working papers and journal articles on value, momentum, carry, defensive and trading costs. Many factor papers in the Papers list originate here. Backs Factor investing fundamentals · Momentum.
    • [Verified] Asness, C. (n.d.). Cliff's Perspectives. AQR Capital Management. https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Perspectives (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · A founder's essays, often on value's drawdown, factor timing and critics of quant. Read them as the strongest statement of one side of contested debates. Backs Factor timing, crowding and quant winters.
    • [Verified] Optiver (n.d.). Insights and explainers. https://www.optiver.com/insights/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Plain-English explainers from a market maker on market making, options pricing, the Greeks and ETFs, plus market-structure opinion pieces. Backs Options market making · ETF market making.
    • [Verified] IMC Trading (n.d.). Blogs. https://www.imc.com/us/blogs (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Mostly careers and culture, with occasional research-team write-ups from ML conferences. It is good for understanding roles and teams, thin on markets.
    • [Reported] Orosz, G., Klaric, I. & Spevack, J. (2026-08-11). "Software engineering at a proprietary trading company: Optiver." The Pragmatic Engineer. https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/optiver (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · A reported profile of how a market maker's engineering organization works. Backs Trading and research tech stacks · Roles, teams and careers.

    Independent blogs, aggregators and communities¶

    • [Verified] Quantocracy (n.d.). Quant Mashup. https://quantocracy.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · A daily aggregator of quant blog posts and papers. Quality varies from excellent to retail backtest spam, so filter by author. Backs Sanity-checking claims.
    • [Verified] Alpha Architect (n.d.). Blog. https://alphaarchitect.com/blog/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Accessible reviews of academic factor and anomaly papers. The firm also issues factor ETFs, so weigh the framing. Backs Factor investing fundamentals.
    • [Verified] Chen, A. Y. & Zimmermann, T. (n.d.). Open Source Asset Pricing. https://www.openassetpricing.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Code and data reproducing more than 300 published cross-sectional predictors, with release notes that document fixes, including an October 2024 look-ahead fix. It is the fastest way to check a factor claim yourself. Backs Factor investing fundamentals · Feature engineering.
    • [Verified] Carver, R. (n.d.). This Blog is Systematic. https://qoppac.blogspot.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · A practitioner's long-running blog on systematic futures trading: position sizing, costs, forecast combination and backtest hygiene, with open-source code. Backs Position sizing and risk budgeting · Alpha combination.
    • [Verified] Chan, E. P. (n.d.). Quantitative Trading blog. https://epchan.blogspot.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The author of two books on the Books list writes here on mean reversion, ML and regime ideas, with a retail-to-small-fund perspective. Backs Pairs trading and cointegration.
    • [Verified] Abdelmessih, K. (n.d.). Moontower. https://moontower.substack.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Options and volatility intuition from a former options trader: why vol is the traded asset, how market makers think about edge and risk. Backs Volatility risk premium and vol arb · Options market making.
    • [Verified] Mechanical Markets (n.d.). Blog. https://mechanicalmarkets.wordpress.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · An infrequent but sharp blog on HFT economics and market-structure proposals such as retail order auctions. Backs Retail flow internalization · Latency arbitrage.
    • [Verified] López de Prado, M. (n.d.). Quantitative Research (papers and slides). https://www.quantresearch.org/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Free versions of the author's papers and lecture slides on backtest overfitting, HRP and financial ML. Backs Overfitting and multiple testing.
    • [Verified] Quantpedia (n.d.). Encyclopedia of quantitative trading strategies. https://quantpedia.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · A catalogue of published strategies with summaries and paper links (partly paywalled). It is useful as an index, but its backtests are not evidence. Backs How quants evaluate a strategy.
    • [Verified] Cboe Global Markets (n.d.). Insights. https://www.cboe.com/insights/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Exchange commentary and monthly volume statistics for options and VIX products, including 0DTE share. It is a primary source for options-market numbers. Backs Options market making.
    • [Verified] Quantitative Finance Stack Exchange (n.d.). https://quant.stackexchange.com/ (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Q&A where practitioners answer pricing, risk and microstructure questions. Good answers are excellent, but check the votes and the author. Backs Questions to ask.
    • [Verified] arXiv (n.d.). Quantitative Finance: Trading and Market Microstructure (q-fin.TR), recent submissions. https://arxiv.org/list/q-fin.TR/recent (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Where microstructure, execution and market-making preprints appear first. Preprints are unrefereed. Backs Order-book and trade-flow signals.
