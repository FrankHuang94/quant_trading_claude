    Papers¶

    TL;DR

    • 120 papers and official reports, grouped by strategy family and pipeline stage. They include every paper the build spec requires and the papers the strategy dossiers and algorithm cards cite most.
    • Each entry gives what the paper shows, how hard it is (★ to ★★★) and which KB pages rest on it. Bibliographic details were checked against Crossref (DOI registry) metadata in 2026-11.
    • If you read ten, read Kyle; Glosten & Milgrom; Avellaneda & Stoikov; Avellaneda & Lee; Khandani & Lo; Jegadeesh & Titman; McLean & Pontiff; Gu, Kelly & Xiu; Almgren & Chriss; and Grinold's Fundamental Law.
    • Academic backtests are gross of most real costs and usually decayed after publication. Treat every Sharpe ratio below as an upper bound until a dossier says otherwise.

    Level: Deep dive · Read time: ~25 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    How to read an entry. ★ means the introduction and results tables are readable without the math. ★★ needs regression and CFA-level statistics. ★★★ needs stochastic control, optimization theory or heavy econometrics. Within each section, papers run roughly from foundational to recent. Where a dossier quotes a figure from a paper, the figure and its confidence tag live on that dossier; the notes here only orient you.

    Market making and microstructure models¶

    • [Verified] Ho, T. & Stoll, H. R. (1981). "Optimal dealer pricing under transactions and return uncertainty." Journal of Financial Economics 9(1), 47–73. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(81)90020-9 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · The first inventory model of dealing: quotes shift away from the dealer's position, and the spread widens with risk and horizon. Every reservation-price model descends from it. Backs Quoting and inventory control.
    • [Verified] Glosten, L. R. & Milgrom, P. R. (1985). "Bid, ask and transaction prices in a specialist market with heterogeneously informed traders." Journal of Financial Economics 14(1), 71–100. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(85)90044-3 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Shows that a spread can exist from adverse selection alone: the bid and ask are the expected value conditional on a sell or a buy. It is the theory behind every markout. Backs Adverse selection and markouts · Equity market making.
    • [Verified] Kyle, A. S. (1985). "Continuous auctions and insider trading." Econometrica 53(6), 1315–1335. https://doi.org/10.2307/1913210 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · An informed trader hides in noise flow, and prices move linearly in net order flow with slope λ ("Kyle's lambda"). It is the root of linear impact and order-flow signals. Backs Order-book and trade-flow signals · The limit order book.
    • [Verified] Grossman, S. J. & Miller, M. H. (1988). "Liquidity and Market Structure." Journal of Finance 43(3), 617–633. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1988.tb04594.x (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Liquidity is the price of immediacy: market makers absorb temporary imbalances and are paid through the later price reversal. This is the economic rationale for liquidity provision and reversal strategies. Backs Auctions and closing liquidity · Short-term reversal.
    • [Verified] Avellaneda, M. & Stoikov, S. (2008). "High-frequency trading in a limit order book." Quantitative Finance 8(3), 217–224. https://doi.org/10.1080/14697680701381228 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · The canonical quoting model: a reservation price skewed by inventory, plus an optimal spread under exponential fill intensities. Short and readable for its class. Backs Quoting and inventory control · Lab 05.
    • [Verified] Stoikov, S. & Sağlam, M. (2009). "Option market making under inventory risk." Review of Derivatives Research 12(1), 55–79. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11147-009-9036-3 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Extends inventory-aware quoting to options, where risk is held in Greeks rather than shares. Backs Options market making.
    • [Verified] Cont, R., Stoikov, S. & Talreja, R. (2010). "A stochastic model for order book dynamics." Operations Research 58(3), 549–563. https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1090.0780 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Models the book as queues with Poisson arrivals and computes the probabilities that matter for placement: the next mid move, and a fill before the price moves. Backs Queue position and order placement.
    • [Verified] Guéant, O., Lehalle, C.-A. & Fernandez-Tapia, J. (2013). "Dealing with the inventory risk: a solution to the market making problem." Mathematics and Financial Economics 7(4), 477–507. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11579-012-0087-0 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · The GLFT closed-form approximation: stationary quotes with inventory limits and no terminal-time artefact, as used in practice-style quoting. Backs Quoting and inventory control.
    • [Verified] Cont, R., Kukanov, A. & Stoikov, S. (2014). "The Price Impact of Order Book Events." Journal of Financial Econometrics 12(1), 47–88. https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbt003 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Defines order-flow imbalance and shows short-horizon price changes are roughly linear in it, with slope inversely related to depth. It is the most-used microstructure feature. Backs Order-book and trade-flow signals · Lab 03.
    • [Verified] Huang, W., Lehalle, C.-A. & Rosenbaum, M. (2015). "Simulating and analyzing order book data: The queue-reactive model." Journal of the American Statistical Association 110(509), 107–122. https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2014.982278 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Order arrival and cancel rates depend on current queue sizes. This is a realistic simulator design for testing placement logic. Backs The limit order book.
    • [Verified] Stoikov, S. (2018). "The micro-price: a high-frequency estimator of future prices." Quantitative Finance 18(12), 1959–1966. https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2018.1489139 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Builds a martingale fair-value estimate from imbalance and spread state, fixing the weighted mid's biases. Backs Fair value and microprice · Lab 02.
    • [Verified] Easley, D., López de Prado, M. M. & O'Hara, M. (2012). "Flow toxicity and liquidity in a high-frequency world." Review of Financial Studies 25(5), 1457–1493. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs053 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Introduces VPIN, a volume-clock measure of flow toxicity, and argues it rose before the 2010 Flash Crash. Read it alongside the rebuttal below. Backs Order-book and trade-flow signals.
    • [Verified] Andersen, T. G. & Bondarenko, O. (2014). "VPIN and the flash crash." Journal of Financial Markets 17, 1–46. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.05.005 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · The main critique: VPIN's apparent warning depends on trade-classification and volume choices and has little incremental predictive power. The debate is unresolved; the authors' rejoinder is in the same issue. Backs Order-book and trade-flow signals.
    • [Verified] Kolm, P. N., Turiel, J. & Westray, N. (2023). "Deep order flow imbalance: Extracting alpha at multiple horizons from the limit order book." Mathematical Finance 33(4), 1044–1081. https://doi.org/10.1111/mafi.12413 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Deep nets fed multi-level OFI beat nets fed raw book states. Feature design still matters with deep learning. Backs Fair value and microprice · Neural nets and sequence models.

    HFT, market design and retail flow (empirical)¶

    • [Verified] Menkveld, A. J. (2013). "High frequency trading and the new market makers." Journal of Financial Markets 16(4), 712–740. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.06.006 (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The cleanest real HFT market-maker P&L: one firm's spread revenue minus positioning losses, per trade. It is the template for this KB's market-maker income statement. Backs Equity market making · Adverse selection and markouts.
    • [Verified] Brogaard, J., Hendershott, T. & Riordan, R. (2014). "High-Frequency Trading and Price Discovery." Review of Financial Studies 27(8), 2267–2306. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhu032 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · On aggregate, HFTs trade in the direction of permanent price changes and against transitory errors. Their passive fills are adversely selected. Backs Adverse selection and markouts.
    • [Verified] Budish, E., Cramton, P. & Shim, J. (2015). "The High-Frequency Trading Arms Race: Frequent Batch Auctions as a Market Design Response." Quarterly Journal of Economics 130(4), 1547–1621. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjv027 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Shows that continuous limit order books create mechanical arbitrage races, for example ES vs SPY correlation breaking down at millisecond horizons, and proposes frequent batch auctions. Backs Latency arbitrage · ETF and index arbitrage.
    • [Verified] Kirilenko, A., Kyle, A. S., Samadi, M. & Tuzun, T. (2017). "The Flash Crash: High-Frequency Trading in an Electronic Market." Journal of Finance 72(3), 967–998. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12498 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Audit-trail evidence from E-mini futures: HFTs did not trigger the crash, but their usual inventory behavior amplified selling pressure. Backs Strategy failure gallery.
    • [Verified] Baron, M., Brogaard, J., Hagströmer, B. & Kirilenko, A. (2019). "Risk and Return in High-Frequency Trading." Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis 54(3), 993–1024. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109018001096 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · HFT profits concentrate in a few firms, aggressive styles earn the most, and relative speed predicts returns. This is evidence for winner-take-most economics. Backs Equity market making · Evolution of quant strategies.
    • [Verified] van Kervel, V. & Menkveld, A. J. (2019). "High-frequency trading around large institutional orders." Journal of Finance 74(3), 1091–1137. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12759 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · HFTs first lean against large institutional orders, then trade with them as the orders persist, raising execution costs. This is why execution algos hide intent. Backs Execution algorithms overview.
    • [Verified] Shkilko, A. & Sokolov, K. (2020). "Every Cloud Has a Silver Lining: Fast Trading, Microwave Connectivity, and Trading Costs." Journal of Finance 75(6), 2899–2927. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12969 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · A natural experiment: rain disrupts microwave links, latency arbitrage drops, and liquidity improves. It is causal evidence that speed races tax liquidity providers. Backs Latency arbitrage.
    • [Verified] Aquilina, M., Budish, E. & O'Neill, P. (2022). "Quantifying the High-Frequency Trading 'Arms Race'." Quarterly Journal of Economics 137(1), 493–564. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjab032 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Uses LSE message data, including failed orders, to measure races directly: their share of volume, the implied latency tax, and how concentrated the winners are. Backs Latency arbitrage · The big picture.
    • [Verified] Battalio, R., Corwin, S. A. & Jennings, R. (2016). "Can Brokers Have It All? On the Relation between Make-Take Fees and Limit Order Execution Quality." Journal of Finance 71(5), 2193–2238. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12422 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Brokers that route limit orders to high-rebate venues get worse fill rates and worse fill quality. This is a maker-taker conflict of interest. Backs Fee- and microstructure-driven strategies · Smart order routing.
    • [Verified] Boehmer, E., Jones, C. M., Zhang, X. & Zhang, X. (2021). "Tracking Retail Investor Activity." Journal of Finance 76(5), 2249–2305. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13033 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Identifies retail trades in public TRF prints by their subpenny price improvement, and finds that retail order imbalance predicts returns. Later work questions the classifier's accuracy. Backs Retail flow internalization.
    • [Verified] Bryzgalova, S., Pavlova, A. & Sikorskaya, T. (2023). "Retail Trading in Options and the Rise of the Big Three Wholesalers." Journal of Finance 78(6), 3465–3514. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13285 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Documents the retail options boom, its concentration in cheap short-dated contracts, and the three wholesalers that buy most of the flow. Backs Options market making.
    • [Verified] Schwarz, C., Barber, B. M., Huang, X., Jorion, P. & Odean, T. (2025). "The 'Actual Retail Price' of Equity Trades." Journal of Finance 80(5), 2507–2541. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13467 (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The authors placed real trades across brokers and found that execution prices differ materially by broker, which matters more than the PFOF headline. Backs Retail flow internalization.
    • [Verified] Bogousslavsky, V. & Muravyev, D. (2023). "Who trades at the close? Implications for price discovery and liquidity." Journal of Financial Markets 66, 100852. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2023.100852 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · As closing-auction volume has grown, closing prices carry temporary distortions that partly reverse overnight. Backs Auctions and closing liquidity · Intraday patterns.

    ETFs, index events and flows¶

    • [Verified] Harris, L. & Gurel, E. (1986). "Price and Volume Effects Associated with Changes in the S&P 500 List: New Evidence for the Existence of Price Pressures." Journal of Finance 41(4), 815–829. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1986.tb04550.x (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The classic index-inclusion result: an announcement pop that partly reverses, i.e., price pressure from index demand. Backs Flow-driven strategies.
    • [Verified] Petajisto, A. (2017). "Inefficiencies in the pricing of exchange-traded funds." Financial Analysts Journal 73(1), 24–54. https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v73.n1.7 (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · ETF premiums and discounts to NAV are larger where the underlying is hard to price or trade, and arbitrage closes them only partly. Backs ETF market making · ETF and index arbitrage.
    • [Verified] Ben-David, I., Franzoni, F. & Moussawi, R. (2018). "Do ETFs increase volatility?" Journal of Finance 73(6), 2471–2535. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12727 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Creation and redemption arbitrage passes ETF-level liquidity shocks into the underlying stocks and raises their volatility. Backs ETF and index arbitrage.
    • [Verified] Greenwood, R. & Sammon, M. (2025). "The Disappearing Index Effect." Journal of Finance 80(2), 657–698. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13410 (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The S&P 500 inclusion premium shrank from the 1990s to almost nothing as the trade became crowded and anticipated. It is a model case of alpha decay. Backs Flow-driven strategies.

    Statistical arbitrage and short-horizon reversal¶

    • [Verified] Lehmann, B. N. (1990). "Fads, Martingales, and Market Efficiency." Quarterly Journal of Economics 105(1), 1–28. https://doi.org/10.2307/2937816 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Weekly contrarian portfolios earn large gross profits, but bid-ask bounce and costs eat much of them. This is the origin of the short-term reversal literature. Backs Short-term reversal.
    • [Verified] Jegadeesh, N. (1990). "Evidence of Predictable Behavior of Security Returns." Journal of Finance 45(3), 881–898. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb05110.x (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Documents one-month reversal alongside longer-horizon continuation, the twin patterns behind reversal and momentum books. Backs Short-term reversal · Stat arb overview.
    • [Verified] Lo, A. W. & MacKinlay, A. C. (1990). "When Are Contrarian Profits Due to Stock Market Overreaction?" Review of Financial Studies 3(2), 175–205. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/3.2.175 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Decomposes contrarian profits and finds that a large share comes from lead-lag cross-autocorrelation (large stocks lead small), not only from overreaction. Backs Stat arb overview.
    • [Verified] Campbell, J. Y., Grossman, S. J. & Wang, J. (1993). "Trading Volume and Serial Correlation in Stock Returns." Quarterly Journal of Economics 108(4), 905–939. https://doi.org/10.2307/2118454 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Price moves on high volume tend to reverse, consistent with pay for liquidity provision. Volume-conditioned reversal signals come from here. Backs Factor-residual mean reversion.
    • [Verified] Gatev, E., Goetzmann, W. N. & Rouwenhorst, K. G. (2006). "Pairs Trading: Performance of a Relative-Value Arbitrage Rule." Review of Financial Studies 19(3), 797–827. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhj020 (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The distance-method pairs benchmark on 1962–2002 data. Read it for the method and the committed-capital accounting. Backs Pairs trading and cointegration · Lab 08.
    • [Verified] Khandani, A. E. & Lo, A. W. (2007). "What Happened to the Quants in August 2007?" Journal of Investment Management 5(4). https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/august07.pdf (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · Written weeks after the event. A simple contrarian strategy reproduces the losses of the week of August 6, which fits a forced deleveraging of crowded books. Backs Strategy failure gallery.
    • [Verified] Avellaneda, M. & Lee, J.-H. (2010). "Statistical arbitrage in the US equities market." Quantitative Finance 10(7), 761–782. https://doi.org/10.1080/14697680903124632 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · The reference design for PCA/ETF-residual stat arb: fit residuals as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process and trade the s-score. Its Sharpe ratios decay over the sample. Backs Factor-residual mean reversion · Lab 09.
    • [Verified] Heston, S. L., Korajczyk, R. A. & Sadka, R. (2010). "Intraday Patterns in the Cross-section of Stock Returns." Journal of Finance 65(4), 1369–1407. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · A stock's return in a given half-hour predicts its return in the same half-hour on later days. Periodic flows leave footprints. Backs Intraday patterns and seasonality.
    • [Verified] Khandani, A. E. & Lo, A. W. (2011). "What happened to the quants in August 2007? Evidence from factors and transactions data." Journal of Financial Markets 14(1), 1–46. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2010.07.005 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · The journal version adds factor and transaction data: liquidity provision evaporated and losses spread through common positions. This is the KB's most-cited crowding paper. Backs Factor-residual mean reversion · Factor timing, crowding and quant winters.
    • [Verified] Nagel, S. (2012). "Evaporating Liquidity." Review of Financial Studies 25(7), 2005–2039. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs066 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Treats reversal-strategy returns as the return to liquidity provision. They spike when VIX is high, which is when liquidity providers retreat. Backs Short-term reversal.
    • [Verified] Da, Z., Liu, Q. & Schaumburg, E. (2014). "A Closer Look at the Short-Term Return Reversal." Management Science 60(3), 658–674. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1766 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Strip out industry moves and cash-flow news, and the residual reversal is much stronger. This is the case for residualizing before trading reversal. Backs Short-term reversal · Lab 10.
    • [Verified] Krauss, C., Do, X. A. & Huck, N. (2017). "Deep neural networks, gradient-boosted trees, random forests: Statistical arbitrage on the S&P 500." European Journal of Operational Research 259(2), 689–702. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2016.10.031 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★ · Daily ML stat arb with high pre-cost returns that shrink sharply in later years. A cautionary benchmark. Backs ML-driven short-horizon alpha.
    • [Verified] Gao, L., Han, Y., Li, S. Z. & Zhou, G. (2018). "Market intraday momentum." Journal of Financial Economics 129(2), 394–414. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.05.009 (accessed 2026-11) — ★ · The first half-hour's market return predicts the last half-hour's. Backs Intraday patterns and seasonality.
    • [Verified] Guijarro-Ordonez, J., Pelger, M. & Zanotti, G. (2026). "Deep Learning Statistical Arbitrage." Management Science 72(9), 7502–7549. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2022.03132 (accessed 2026-11) — ★★★ · Learns both the residual portfolios and the trading policy end to end with a CNN-transformer. It is the frontier version of A&L, with academic, pre-capacity Sharpe ratios. Backs Factor-residual mean reversion · Stat arb overview.

    Factors and anomalies¶

    • [Verified] Fama, E. F. & MacBeth, J. D. (1973). "Risk, Return, and Equilibrium: Empirical Tests."
