    One-page cheat sheet¶

    TL;DR

    • One pipeline, many clock speeds: data → signals → forecast → portfolio → orders → fills → P&L, run in microseconds by a market maker and monthly by a factor manager.
    • Ask of any strategy: who pays, and why do they accept it? Of any Sharpe ratio: gross or net, over what period, backtest or live?
    • Below: ten formulas, twelve numbers (as of 2026-11) and ten misused terms, each linked to its explaining page.

    Level: Core · Read time: ~8 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    The pipeline in one line¶

    Data → signals → forecast → portfolio → orders → fills → P&L → feedback to signals. A market maker closes the loop in microseconds, with "portfolio" shrunk to inventory skew; a factor manager closes it monthly (There is no single algorithm). Where the formulas attach:

    flowchart LR
    S["Signals<br/>1 IC"] --> F["Forecast<br/>2 Fundamental Law"]
    F --> P["Portfolio<br/>4 breakeven cost, 8 Kelly, 9 vol target"]
    P --> O["Orders<br/>5 square-root impact, 6 Almgren-Chriss"]
    O --> X["Fills"]
    X --> L["PnL<br/>3 Sharpe standard error"]
    L -.->|"feedback"| S
    MM["Market-maker loop<br/>7 Avellaneda-Stoikov, 10 gamma PnL"] -.->|"quotes are its orders"| O

    Firm types × horizon × edge¶

    Firm type Horizon Main edge Who pays Start with
    Electronic market makers µs to minutes liquidity provision · speed traders paying the half-spread Equity MM
    HFT prop (aggressive) µs to seconds speed · structural stale quoters Latency arbitrage
    Quant hedge funds minutes to weeks informational · behavioral slower or constrained investors Stat arb
    Multi-manager pods hours to weeks informational · behavioral the same, market-neutral, under tight drawdown limits Factor-residual reversion
    Factor managers months risk premium · behavioral investors shedding disliked risks Factor investing
    CTAs weeks to months behavioral · risk premium under-reacting traders and hedgers Trend following
    Agency execution an order's life none sought commissions Execution algorithms

    Ten formulas¶

    # Formula What it says Page
    1 IC: \(IC_t = \mathrm{corr}_{\mathrm{rank}}(s_{i,t},\ r_{i,t+h})\) Daily cross-sectional score of signal vs forward return; noise ≈ \(1/\sqrt{N}\) (0.045 at 500 names). Mean ÷ sd × √252 ≈ annual IR D4
    2 Fundamental Law: \(IR \approx TC \cdot IC \cdot \sqrt{BR}\) Skill × √(independent bets a year) × what constraints let through (TC 0.3–0.8). IC 0.01 on 50,000 bets: IR ≈ 2.2 before costs Evaluating a strategy
    3 Sharpe SE: \(SE(\widehat{SR}) \approx \sqrt{(1 + SR^2/2)/T}\) Per-period units (Lo 2002). From daily data, t ≈ SR × √years: a true 0.75 needs ~7 years to reach t = 2 V4
    4 Breakeven cost: \(c^* = \alpha/T\), \(\alpha_{net} = \alpha - Tc\) 12% gross alpha at 60× turnover breaks even at 20 bp per dollar traded; each extra bp of cost costs 0.6% a year Evaluating a strategy
    5 Square-root impact: \(I \approx Y \sigma \sqrt{Q/V}\) Y ≈ 0.5–1, σ daily. 5% of ADV in a 2%-vol stock: 22–45 bp; double the order, +41% per share P5
    6 Almgren-Chriss: \(\min\ E[IS] + \lambda \mathrm{Var}[IS]\); \(x(t) = X \frac{\sinh \kappa (T-t)}{\sinh \kappa T}\), \(\kappa \approx \sqrt{\lambda \sigma^2/\eta}\) Impact punishes speed, risk punishes waiting; λ = 0 is TWAP E3
    7 Avellaneda-Stoikov: \(r = s - q\gamma\sigma^2(T-t)\); spread \(\gamma\sigma^2(T-t) + \frac{2}{\gamma}\ln(1 + \frac{\gamma}{k})\) The reservation price leans against inventory: long (q > 0) shades both quotes down M4
    8 Kelly: \(f^* = \mu/\sigma^2\); growth \(f\mu - f^2\sigma^2/2\) 7% excess return at 10% vol: 7× leverage. Half Kelly: 75% of the growth at half the vol P7
    9 Vol targeting: \(w_t = \sigma^{*}/\hat{\sigma}_t\) Scale exposure by target ÷ forecast vol (EWMA); 10% a year is a common target [Lore] P7
    10 Gamma P&L: \(d\Pi \approx \frac{1}{2}\Gamma S^2(\sigma^2_{impl} - \sigma^2_{real})\,dt\) A delta-hedged short option earns theta, pays gamma: a bet that implied variance exceeds realized. Long side: flip the sign M7

    Ten terms people misuse¶

    Term Common misuse What a quant means
    Alpha Any outperformance A forecast of residual return; "an alpha" is one signal
    Statistical arbitrage Riskless profit A diversified bet on relative mispricing; it can lose badly (2007 quant quake)
    HFT One strategy, usually front-running A speed regime: the SEC sorts HFT into passive market making, arbitrage, structural and directional [Verified]
    Front-running Trading ahead of public, predictable flows Trading ahead of a known client order (FINRA Rule 5270 covers imminent blocks) [Verified]; trading announced index changes is not
    Sharpe ratio One comparable number Meaningless without gross or net, period and backtest or live
    Out-of-sample Any data not used to fit Data never used to fit or choose the model; a peeked-at holdout is in-sample
    Liquidity Volume The cost of trading your size now: spread, depth and how fast the book refills
    Dark pool Shady, or all off-exchange trading An ATS without displayed quotes: ~19% of off-exchange volume; wholesalers do most of the rest [Reported]
    PFOF Paying to trade against customers Wholesalers pay for uninformed retail flow and fill it inside the NBBO; legal in the US, banned in the EU (exemptions ended 2026-06-30) [Reported]
    Market neutral Dollar neutral Beta-neutral and factor-neutral too; dollar-neutral books can hide factor bets
