    Faux pas and naive questions¶

    TL;DR

    • Most naive questions fail in one of three ways: they fish for the secret (signals, positions, P&L), they assert a myth (HFT front-runs, AI predicts stocks), or they ask for one number stripped of its conditions (Sharpe, hit rate, speed).
    • Each has a fix: ask for the method, ask about the evidence, or ask for the conditions (gross or net, period, capacity).
    • Below are 24 common ones, each with why it lands badly and a better question that shows you know the mechanics.

    Level: Core · Read time: ~6 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    Three ways to fail, three repairs¶

    flowchart LR
    N1["Fishes for the secret<br/>what, which, how much"] --> R1["Ask for the method<br/>how decided, how measured"]
    N2["Asserts a myth<br/>HFT front-runs, AI predicts"] --> R2["Ask about the evidence<br/>what the data show"]
    N3["Wants one number<br/>Sharpe, hit rate, speed"] --> R3["Ask for its conditions<br/>gross or net, period, capacity"]

    None of these is offensive; they mark you as an outsider, and answers drop to the question's level. See also questions to ask and myths vs reality.

    Fishing for the secret¶

    Naive Why it lands badly Better
    "What's your algorithm?" There is no single algorithm: a firm runs dozens of models across a pipeline (no single algorithm), all proprietary. "Where does most of your edge come from: signal, portfolio construction or execution?"
    "What's your Sharpe?" Confidential, and meaningless without gross or net, period and capacity. "What net Sharpe is realistic for this kind of strategy, and how fast does it fall with size?" (evaluating a strategy)
    "What are you long right now?" Positions are the strategy, and discussing them raises compliance flags. "How concentrated is a typical book: number of names, largest position as a share of capital?" (sizing)
    "How much did your desk make?" Desk P&L is the most guarded number in the building. "In a normal year, how does P&L split between spread, rebates and inventory?" (attribution)
    "Which lookback do you use?" Parameters are the strategy; firms litigate over less (Jane Street v. Millennium, settled 2024 [Reported]). "How sensitive are results to the lookback; do you blend several speeds?" (trend following)
    "Where's the market going?" Most quant books hedge out market direction; they have no house view. "Which market conditions hurt your strategy class most?" (regime detection)

    Misreading the business¶

    Naive Why it lands badly Better
    "HFT is front-running, right?" Front-running means trading ahead of a customer order you hold, which FINRA Rule 5320 bars [Verified]; market makers react to public quotes. The fair critique is narrower: HFTs trade with long institutional orders once they detect them [Verified] (van Kervel and Menkveld 2019). "How do you read the evidence that HFTs trade alongside large institutional orders?" (latency arbitrage)
    "Market making is free money: you collect the spread." Adverse selection eats much of it. In Menkveld's data, €1.55 of spread per trade netted €0.88 after €0.68 of position losses [Verified]. "What share of spread capture goes to adverse selection?" (markouts)
    "So you predict whether stocks go up?" Many never forecast direction: market makers sell immediacy, stat arb is market-neutral, factor funds harvest premia. "Is your edge a forecast, a service, or a premium for bearing risk?" (strategy map)
    "Why not just scale it up?" Market impact grows roughly with the square root of size, so net alpha shrinks as assets grow. "Where does your capacity curve bend?" (cost models)
    "Which hedge fund are you?" (to a prop firm) Proprietary trading firms risk only their own capital and have no outside investors. "Is the capital your own or clients'?" (taxonomy)
    "Renaissance does it; why can't you?" Medallion is closed and capacity-limited. In 2020 it reportedly gained 76% while Renaissance's public RIEF fund lost 19% [Reported]. "What separates capacity-limited strategies from scalable ones?" (Renaissance)

    Statistics and backtests¶

    Naive Why it lands badly Better
    "The backtest Sharpe is 3, so it works." Selection across trials inflates it. Across 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe explained under 2.5% of live Sharpe (R² < 0.025) [Verified]. "How many variants were tried, and what does the deflated Sharpe say?" (overfitting)
    "Why not just buy the backtest?" Backtests are cheap to make and easy to overfit; the value is the data, process and execution that survive live. "What did live trading teach you that the backtest never did?" (backtest pitfalls)
    "A 52% hit rate is barely better than a coin flip." Small edges on many independent bets add up: information ratio ≈ IC × √breadth (fundamental law). "What are your IC and breadth, and how much survives costs?" (evaluating signals)
    "More features must make the model better." With low signal-to-noise, extra features mostly add ways to overfit. "How does a new feature earn its place?" (feature engineering)
    "Why not just use stop-losses?" Stops cost money under a random walk and help only when returns trend (Kaminski and Lo 2014) [Verified]; they hurt mean-reversion books most. "What de-grossing rules do you run, and how did they behave in August 2007?" (quant winters)
    "You lost money last month, so the model is broken." A strategy with an annual Sharpe of 1 loses in about 4 months out of 10 [Estimate] (monthly Sharpe 1/√12 ≈ 0.29; P(loss) ≈ 39%, assuming normal returns). "Was that drawdown inside what your risk model expected?" (alpha decay)

    AI and technology¶

    Naive Why it lands badly Better
    "Can't you just use ChatGPT to predict stocks?" LLM headline scores show modest predictability that shrinks as adoption spreads, and pre-cutoff backtests suffer look-ahead (Lopez-Lira and Tang 2026) [Verified]. "Where do LLMs help you most: signals, research tooling, or both?" (LLMs in quant)
    "Is it all deep learning now?" Regularized linear models and tree ensembles still carry much production alpha in noisy, shifting data [Lore]. "Where do nonlinear models beat ridge for you after costs?" (linear models)
    "How fast are you, nanoseconds?" Latency figures are guarded, and speed matters for only some strategies. "Which of your strategies are latency-sensitive, and which are not?" (HFT loop)
    "Why not run it all in the cloud?" Latency-sensitive trading runs colocated beside exchange engines; cloud and GPU clusters serve research. "What runs colocated, and what goes to your research clusters?" (infrastructure)

    Ethics and reputation¶

    Naive Why it lands badly Better
    "Isn't PFOF a kickback that rips off retail?" States a contested question as fact. Retail orders do get price improvement; the debate is over how much. "What do the new Rule 605 statistics show about retail price improvement?" (retail internalization)
    "Didn't HFTs cause the Flash Crash?" The CFTC-SEC report traced the trigger to a large E-mini sell program; the most active HFTs kept trading as before but did not absorb the selling (Kirilenko et al. 2017) [Verified]. "When the market gaps, do your controls widen, pull or keep quoting?" (risk controls)
