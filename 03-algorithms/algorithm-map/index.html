    Algorithm map¶

    TL;DR

    • All 39 algorithm cards on one diagram: five research-to-trading stages (data and signals → forecasting → validation → portfolio construction → execution), a parallel market-making/HFT lane, and live trading as the loop that feeds results back.
    • Every card has a stable ID (D1…L3). The ID's letter tells you the stage; the five canonical pipelines are different paths through the same map.
    • Latency regimes run from nanosecond risk gates to research jobs that take hours. A card's regime tells you what kind of engineering it needs.
    • The map is not a one-way conveyor. Three feedback loops matter: TCA recalibrates the cost model, live monitoring retires decaying alphas, and markouts retune quoting.

    Level: Core · Read time: ~7 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    How to read the map¶

    Solid arrows show what feeds what on a normal trading day; dotted arrows show research gates and feedback. Validation cards (V) never touch live orders; they decide whether a forecast reaches production. The market-making lane (M) is a separate, much faster loop that shares venues, risk controls and monitoring with the main pipeline. M9 Algorithmic risk controls sits in that lane, but every live order in every pipeline passes a pre-trade risk check.

    The 39 cards¶

    Latency regimes are typical orders of magnitude, not firm-specific measurements [Estimate] (reasoning: a market-making critical path must finish between microsecond-spaced book updates; a daily rebalance only has to finish overnight). Pipelines: 1 HFT/market making, 2 short-horizon stat arb, 3 mid-frequency, 4 factor, 5 agency execution.

    ID Card Stage One-line purpose Latency regime Pipelines
    D1 Point-in-time data and cleaning data Make every backtest see only what was knowable then overnight batch 2, 3, 4
    D2 Feature engineering signal Turn prices, fundamentals, book data and text into predictors daily batch to streaming 2, 3, 4
    D3 Signal transforms signal Winsorize, rank, z-score, neutralize same cadence as features 2, 3, 4
    D4 Evaluating signals signal IC, decay, turnover and capacity before a signal gets capital offline research 2, 3, 4
    F1 Linear models and regularization forecast Ridge and lasso: the robust baseline train offline; predict µs–ms 1, 2, 3
    F2 Tree ensembles forecast Boosted trees for nonlinear interactions train minutes; predict µs per stock 2, 3
    F3 Neural nets and sequence models forecast Learn from raw sequences when data are plentiful GPU training; ms inference 1, 2
    F4 LLMs and foundation models in quant forecast Turn text into signals overnight or event-driven; seconds per document 3
    F5 Alpha combination and ensembling forecast Blend many signals into one forecast per stock daily, sometimes intraday 2, 3, 4
    F6 Regime detection forecast Detect volatility regimes to scale risk daily 3, 4
    V1 Time-series cross-validation validation Purged, embargoed CV that respects time order offline research 2, 3
    V2 Backtesting engines validation Simulate with realistic fills, costs and constraints offline research 2, 3, 4
    V3 Backtest pitfalls validation Look-ahead, survivorship, data snooping offline research 2, 3, 4
    V4 Overfitting and multiple testing validation Deflated Sharpe, PBO, higher t-hurdles offline research 2, 3, 4

    P4 Alternatives to MVO portfolio Risk parity, HRP, heuristics when MVO is fragile seconds 3, 4
    P5 Transaction cost and impact models portfolio Predict spread plus impact for a trade fit overnight; µs per name 2, 3, 4, 5
    P6 Multi-period optimization and trading toward the aim portfolio Trade partway toward a target that anticipates decay per rebalance 2, 3, 4

    E1 Execution algorithms overview execution Pick the algo family for a parent order per order; re-planned every 1–5 min 2, 3, 5

    E6 Transaction cost analysis execution Realized cost versus benchmarks post-trade, daily 3, 5

    The feedback loops¶

    Solid arrows explain one day; dotted arrows explain why a pipeline gets better or worse over months.

    flowchart LR
  fills["Fills and child orders"] --> E6["E6 TCA"]
  E6 -->|"realized impact"| P5["P5 Cost model"]
  P5 -->|"trade more or less"| P3["P3 Optimizer"]
  P5 -->|"faster or slower"| E3["E3 Schedule"]
  pnl["Daily PnL"] --> L1["L1 Attribution"]
  L1 -->|"PnL by signal"| L2["L2 Decay tests"]
  L2 -->|"retire or down-weight"| F5["F5 Alpha combination"]
  L2 -->|"research priorities"| D2["D2 Features"]
  mk["Fills in the MM lane"] --> M5["M5 Markouts"]
  M5 -->|"toxicity by venue"| M4["M4 Quote width and skew"]
  M5 -->|"refit"| M2["M2 Fair value"]
    1. Execution cost loop. TCA feeds realized market impact into the cost model. If impact runs above forecast, the optimizer trades less and schedules slow down. Without it, capacity estimates are guesses.
    2. Alpha lifecycle loop. Attribution splits P&L by signal; decay tests decide when live IC has fallen far enough below the backtest to cut a signal's weight, freeing risk budget for new research.
    3. Adverse-selection loop. Markouts expose adverse selection by venue and counterparty; quoting widens or skews and the fair-value model is refit.
