    Strategies: the map¶

    TL;DR

    • This module has 30 strategy dossiers in seven families (A–G), plus five cross-cutting pages (H). Every dossier follows one template, so any two compare directly.
    • Every strategy sits at a point on two axes: holding horizon (microseconds to months) and edge source (speed · liquidity provision · structural/regulatory · informational · behavioral · risk premium).
    • Short horizons are dominated by liquidity provision, speed and structure; long horizons by risk premia and behavioral effects. Informational edges sit in the middle.
    • Most "stat arb" is liquidity provision on a slower clock: you are paid for absorbing other people's price pressure.
    • Read every dossier's header table first. If you only remember one row, make it "Counterparty: who pays and why they accept it."

    Level: Core · Read time: ~9 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    Three lessons. First, horizon and edge are correlated, not identical: liquidity provision runs from microseconds (quoting) to weeks (pairs), because absorbing price pressure is paid at every horizon (Grossman and Miller; Nagel) [Verified]. Second, capacity rises with horizon while Sharpe falls; the comparison matrix puts numbers on it. Third, the middle is the most crowded: days-to-weeks informational and behavioral signals are where most quant funds and pods compete, and where published signals decay fastest (evaluation).

    All 30 dossiers¶

    Family Dossier Horizon Edge source Who pays you, in one line
    A · Liquidity Equity market making µs to minutes LP · speed Traders who want to deal now pay the half-spread; waiting costs them more
    A · Liquidity ETF market making µs to hours LP · structural ETF buyers and sellers pay a spread over basket value rather than create or redeem themselves
    A · Liquidity Options market making minutes to days LP · speed Hedgers, income sellers and retail pay edge over theoretical value for convexity or leverage
    A · Liquidity Retail flow internalization seconds to minutes LP · structural Retail orders pay part of the spread: filled inside the NBBO but away from mid; the flow is valuable because it is uninformed
    A · Liquidity Auctions and closing liquidity overnight to a few days LP · structural Index funds and benchmarked managers who must trade at the close concede price to imbalance absorbers
    B · Speed Latency arbitrage µs to seconds speed · structural Slower liquidity providers whose stale quotes get picked off
    B · Speed Lead-lag and cross-asset arbitrage ms to minutes speed · info Quoters in the lagging instrument (stocks after futures move)
    B · Speed ETF and index arbitrage ms to a day structural · speed Whoever pushes an ETF or future away from its basket value
    B · Speed Fee- and microstructure-driven ms to minutes structural · LP Takers paying access fees; traders disadvantaged by tick and fee rules
    C · Stat arb Stat arb: overview and history hours to days LP · behavioral Liquidity demanders whose trades push prices temporarily away from relative value
    C · Stat arb Pairs trading and cointegration days to weeks LP · behavioral Traders who push one leg away from its twin; you are paid for patience and idiosyncratic risk
    C · Stat arb Factor-residual mean reversion 1 to 15 days LP · behavioral The same price pressure, harvested across hundreds of names after removing factor moves
    C · Stat arb Short-term cross-sectional reversal 1 day to 1 month (classically 1 week) LP · behavioral Impatient sellers and buyers; profits rise when market makers' capital is scarce
    C · Stat arb Intraday patterns and seasonality minutes to hours structural · behavioral Traders with schedule-driven flows (open, close, periodic rebalancing)
    C · Stat arb ML-driven short-horizon alpha minutes to a day info · LP Slower traders whose order flow and reactions are predictable from features
    D · Mid-freq Cross-sectional alpha and neutralization days to weeks behavioral · info Investors who process information slowly or are constrained from acting on it
    D · Mid-freq Fundamental quant signals weeks to months risk premium · behavioral Investors who misread accounting quality, or who demand a premium to hold unloved stocks
    D · Mid-freq Earnings and event-driven days to weeks behavioral · LP Investors who under-react to earnings news (post-earnings drift)
    D · Mid-freq Analyst revisions and sentiment weeks info · behavioral Investors anchored on stale consensus while estimates drift
    D · Mid-freq Flow-driven strategies days to weeks LP · structural Index funds and other mechanical traders who must trade on known dates regardless of price
    D · Mid-freq Alternative data days to weeks info Investors without the data, who learn sales or traffic only at the official print
    D · Mid-freq NLP and LLM signals hours to days info · behavioral Slower readers of filings, calls and news
    E · Factors Factor investing fundamentals months risk premium · behavioral Investors averse to factor risk, or biased against it, who pay a premium to avoid it
    E · Factors Momentum months behavioral · risk premium Investors who under-react to news, then chase; holders forced to dump losers
    E · Factors Value, quality and low volatility months risk premium · behavioral Extrapolators and distress-averse holders (value); leverage-constrained buyers of high beta (low vol)
    E · Factors Factor timing, crowding and quant winters months risk premium Nobody reliably; this dossier is about when the premia stop paying and crowded exits hurt
    F · Vol Volatility risk premium and vol arb days to weeks risk premium · structural Option buyers paying above expected realized volatility for crash protection
    F · Vol Dispersion and correlation trading weeks to months risk premium · structural Index-protection buyers and single-stock overwriters, who leave implied correlation rich
    G · Futures Trend following and CTAs weeks to months behavioral · risk premium Under-reacting then herding traders, and hedgers; paid in bursts during persistent moves
    G · Futures Carry and cross-asset risk premia months risk premium · structural Hedgers and investors paying to offload risk; you hold crash risk in return

    The "who pays" lines are hypotheses each dossier tests against evidence. Anchor papers (reversal as liquidity payment, pairs, post-earnings drift, momentum, betting against beta, variance risk premia, latency races) are under Sources [Verified].

    How to read a dossier¶

    Every dossier opens with this header table:

    Field What it tells you How to read it
    Family Which of the seven groups (A–G) Families share data, infrastructure and failure modes
    Edge source Primary edge first, then secondary The six-way taxonomy is defined in How quants evaluate a strategy
    Holding period Typical life of a position Sets infrastructure, turnover and how fast you learn the strategy is broken
    Counterparty: who pays and why they accept it The economic victim or customer If nobody identifiable pays, assume the backtest is lying
    Universe & instruments What gets traded and hedged Wider universes add breadth; illiquid names add cost
    Data required Inputs, from direct feeds to filings to alt data Drives cost and point-in-time risk
    Core algorithms used The 2–6 central module 03 cards Feeds the strategy × algorithm matrix
    Infra intensity Latency sensitivity and compute Says whether a microsecond, or a GPU cluster, matters
    Capacity (rough $ range) Money one firm can run before costs eat the edge Always tagged, with reasoning; treat as an order of magnitude
    Typical Sharpe (gross vs net) Risk-adjusted return before and after costs Look for period, source type and decay; an academic backtest is an upper bound
    Turnover How often the book trades Turnover × cost per dollar traded = cost drag
    Main risks / failure modes How it loses money Mechanisms plus dated episodes
    Crowding risk How many others hold the same positions Crowded trades fail together, fast
    Firm types that run it Types from the big picture Named firms only when sourced
    Lab A runnable synthetic-data lab, if any Toy markets; each lab lists what it misses

    Confidence tags. [Verified] means a primary source (filing, regulator, paper, exchange document). [Reported] means credible press, survey or practitioner book. [Estimate] means our derivation, with the reasoning shown. [Lore] means widely repeated but unverified. Every Sharpe ratio, capacity or return figure states gross or net, the period, the source type (academic backtest, fund disclosure, practitioner estimate) and known post-publication decay.

    The body always runs: intuition → economic rationale → the trade → worked example → algorithms and pseudocode → implementation → P&L decomposition → evidence → capacity and decay → failures → textbook vs practice → variants → diagram → practitioner talk → myths → questions to ask (🔒 = proprietary territory) → collapsible math.
