    Roles, teams and careers¶

    TL;DR

    • Five core roles: the quant researcher (QR) finds and validates signals, the quant trader (QT) runs strategies live, the quant developer (QD) turns research into production code, the software engineer (SWE) builds shared infrastructure, and the portfolio manager (PM) owns capital and a P&L. Titles vary by firm.
    • Three ways to organize: market makers run product desks on shared technology; pod shops run PM-led teams with their own P&L; centralized funds run signal teams that feed one model.
    • Pay structure follows the org chart. Few figures are public: Jane Street's 2025 pay pool was reportedly $9.38B [Reported], and star pod PMs have been offered packages above $100M [Reported].
    • To place someone quickly, ask who owns the P&L they work on.

    Level: Core · Read time: ~6 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    The roles¶

    Role Owns Produces Judged by
    Quant researcher (QR) hypotheses, signals, models backtests, alpha signals, research notes out-of-sample IC, marginal contribution to the combined forecast, live P&L
    Quant trader (QT) live strategies, parameters, limits, exceptions parameter changes, risk decisions, post-trade reviews P&L, markouts, risk discipline
    Quant developer (QD) the team's code: simulators, feature pipelines, strategy logic research-to-production handoffs, backtesting engines correctness, speed of iteration
    Software engineer (SWE) shared systems: feed handlers, order gateways, FPGAs, data and compute platforms infrastructure every desk uses latency, uptime, throughput (tech stacks)
    Portfolio manager (PM) capital and a P&L positions, risk, hiring Sharpe ratio, drawdown against limits, capacity

    Around them sit risk managers, execution researchers, data engineers who source and clean datasets, and compliance. The lines blur in practice. A market maker's "trader" usually supervises automated systems and often writes code; a QR at a small firm also ships production code [Lore].

    Three ways to organize¶

    Market maker. Teams are cut by product and region: US equities, ETFs, options, futures. Each desk mixes traders, researchers and desk developers. A central group owns exchange gateways, market data and pre-trade risk checks. Strategies run as continuous automated loops (M1); people tune parameters and handle exceptions such as halts, corporate actions and new listings. Desks depend on each other: the ETF desk needs the equity desk's fair values. That is one reason firm-wide bonus pools are common [Lore].

    Pod shop. A PM leads a small team, often a few QRs and a developer [Lore], with its own signals, optimizer and P&L. The platform supplies data, a risk model, execution and financing. Central risk allocates capital and enforces drawdown stops; Millennium reportedly halves a PM's capital after a 5% loss and is likely to part ways near 7.5% [Reported]. The PM is paid a share of the pod's own P&L, commonly said to be 15–20% [Lore], and pays the team from it.

    Centralized quant fund. Research groups are cut by signal type or horizon. Their forecasts feed one alpha combination model, then a portfolio-construction team and an execution team. A core platform owns data and compute. Researchers often see only part of the model [Lore]. Credit is assigned by attribution, which pushes researchers toward signals uncorrelated with what the model already has.

    The trade-off between the last two models is old. Sharpe (1981) and van Binsbergen, Brandt and Koijen (2008) analyze what a firm loses by splitting a portfolio among independent managers [Verified]. Pods buy accountability and independent ideas; one book saves the costs of duplicated hedges and offsetting trades (how firms combine strategies).

    Pay: what is public¶

    Almost nothing is disclosed. The figures below are press reports from unnamed sources.

    Firm type Structure Public data point
    Market maker discretionary bonus from a firm-wide pool [Lore] Jane Street's 2025 pool was reportedly $9.38B, more than double 2024, about $2.68M per employee on roughly 3,500 staff [Reported]; that is about 24% of its reported revenue [Estimate]
    Pod shop formula payout on own pod P&L [Lore] multi-managers have offered star PMs packages above $100M over several years (2025) [Reported]; Bloomberg reported $120M offers in 2023 [Reported]
    Centralized fund discretionary, by attributed contribution [Lore] not disclosed

    An average is not a typical salary: a few large bonuses dominate it. Structure shapes behavior. A shared pool rewards sharing models across desks. A pod payout rewards guarding ideas and hedging every factor, because the PM keeps a share of gains but not of losses (pod economics). Attribution rewards orthogonal signals.

    Using this in conversation¶

    "Who owns the P&L you work on?" places a person in one question. A QR at a centralized fund may never see a dollar figure for their own signal. A pod PM knows theirs to the basis point. A market-making trader thinks in edge per share and markouts. More openers are in the questions to ask page.
