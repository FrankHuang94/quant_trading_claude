    Taxonomy of quant firms¶

    TL;DR

    • Seven kinds of firm trade US equities quantitatively, surrounded by an ecosystem that sells them access, data, leverage and plumbing. Their math overlaps a lot. What separates them is whose capital is at risk and how the decision-makers get paid.
    • Own-capital firms (market makers, HFT prop) are limited by volume and speed, not by money, so they rarely take outside investors. Fee-earning funds are limited by market impact, which makes capacity a negotiation between manager and investor.
    • Each type gravitates to particular strategy families: market makers to liquidity provision, HFT prop to speed and structure, quant funds and pods to stat arb and mid-frequency alpha, asset managers and CTAs to risk premia and trend.
    • The lines blur. In 2025, non-bank liquidity providers earned more from proprietary trading than from market making [Reported].

    Level: Core · Read time: ~7 min · Last verified: 2026-11

    The big picture maps firm types by horizon and edge. This page explains why each sits where it does: capital source sets the horizon, the horizon sets the edge, and pay structure sets behavior.

    Classifications follow public descriptions [Reported]. Volatility strategies (VRP and vol arb, dispersion) sit with options market makers and multi-strategy funds.

    Capital source decides the rest¶

    Own capital. A market maker's revenue is capped by volume and spread, not equity: it turns its balance sheet over many times a day. Jane Street reported members' equity of about $29.9B at end-2024, against $20.5B of net trading revenue that year [Reported]. Outside equity is rare and strategic: Citadel Securities sold a $1.15B minority stake to Sequoia and Paradigm in January 2022 [Reported]. Jane Street borrows instead; it issued $1.35B of senior secured notes in April 2025 [Reported]. Owners keep all the upside, so they protect the franchise.

    Outside investors. A fund's revenue is fees on assets, so it can grow with capital even while the return on each dollar falls. That puts capacity at the center of the manager-investor relationship (economics page). Investors can redeem, so drawdowns threaten the business: pods stop out PMs, and centralized funds cut risk book-wide. Renaissance resolved the conflict by removing outsiders from its Medallion fund, which has been open only to employees since the mid-2000s [Reported]. Economically, Medallion is closer to a prop firm.

    Mandates and commissions. Asset managers are paid on assets and judged against benchmarks, so tracking error binds and they harvest slow, high-capacity premia. Agency desks are paid per share to execute someone else's decision; their scorecard is transaction cost analysis, their conflict the broker's own venues and flow.

    What kills each type¶

    • Market makers fail through adverse selection and operational accidents, not directional bets. Knight Capital lost over $460M in about 45 minutes in 2012 [Verified] (failure gallery).
    • HFT prop fails when it loses the speed race; second place earns almost nothing. Latency races cost global equity markets about $5B a year, mostly won by a few firms [Verified].
    • Quant hedge funds fail through crowding and decay. In August 2007, funds holding similar positions lost together [Verified].
    • Pods fail when supposedly independent PMs draw down at the same time, or when investors balk at pass-through costs (how firms combine strategies).
    • Systematic asset managers fail slowly, through long factor droughts. HML fell 51% from January 2017 to September 2020 [Verified] (quant winters).
    • CTAs bleed in choppy, trendless years (trend dossier).
    • Agency desks fail through fee compression and lost client trust. ITG paid $20.3M in 2015 over a secret desk that used dark-pool subscribers' order information [Verified].

    The ecosystem¶

    Supplier Sells Why it matters for strategies
    Exchanges and ATSs execution, colocation, proprietary feeds, rebates fee tiers set a market maker's unit economics (fees and rebates)
    Data vendors market data, fundamentals, alternative data, risk models point-in-time history is a barrier to entry; MSCI Barra and Axioma (now part of SimCorp) supply standard risk models [Reported]
    Prime brokers leverage, stock borrow, financing, crowding reports borrow cost and availability cap short books (shorting and financing)
    Clearing firms and CCPs clearing, margin volatility turns into same-day margin calls
    Retail brokers order flow, for payment for order flow the raw material of retail internalization
    Technology vendors FPGAs, network cards, microwave links, GPUs the speed race (semiconductor lens)

    Where the lines blur¶

    • Market makers take directional risk. Coalition Greenwich put non-bank liquidity providers' 2025 trading revenue at about $114B: $84.3B from proprietary trading and $30.2B from market making [Reported].
    • One brand, two firms. Citadel, the hedge fund, and Citadel Securities, the market maker, are separate companies [Reported] (profile).
    • One manager, several types. Man Group runs AHL, a trend-following CTA, and Numeric, a systematic equity manager [Reported] (profile).
